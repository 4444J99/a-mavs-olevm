Music, handwriting, typography and image work can share sources and exhibition
space while retaining clear work identities. An album visual that serves an album
can be its rendition; a titled interpretation with an independent concept can be a
new linked work. An album earns an independent repository when its actual code,
assets, collaborators or release cycle require it. A social account is a separate
ongoing editorial commitment, never a mechanical consequence of an album folder.

Start handwritten material as a dedicated room in the evolved exhibition. Preserve
scans with original marks, spacing and corrections; checked transcriptions and
spoken/musical/animated interpretations link back to that page. Unknown authorship,
chronology or transcription stays explicit. Readable still mode remains available
for text-animation experiences. User-authored imports must keep whitespace and
source attribution intact.

Original mixes can support audiovisual editions now. Remaster, remix, newly recorded
vocals, original multitracks and computationally separated stems each receive
separate provenance; no engineering change invents a recovered performance. Each
album develops its own palette, movement, typography, images, pacing and interaction
with shared software only where useful.

The practice stays open across eras of life and skill. Each release still needs an
honest finish line: it expresses the artist's selected present form, works as
presented and can be encountered. Recovering a page, interpreting it, animating it,
pairing music and performing it can yield distinct public encounters after artist
selection. This technical plan is not authored public artistic copy.
